## What should an AI course actually teach you?

Most of what gets sold as an AI course in small cities is a tour of tools. Someone opens ChatGPT, shows you a prompt, opens an image generator, shows you another prompt, and you go home with a certificate and a feeling that you have missed something. You have. The tour is the easy part.

The part that takes real teaching is judgement. Knowing that the first output is usually mediocre and what to change. Knowing when the model has invented a statistic. Knowing which tasks are worth automating and which ones break when you do. That only comes from using AI on work that has a consequence, which is why ours sits on top of live client campaigns instead of practice exercises.

Here is what a serious curriculum covers, and what you should ask about before paying anyone:

- **Prompting that holds up.** Not clever one-liners. Structured prompts with context, examples and constraints, which you can reuse and hand to someone else.
- **Content production at volume.** Drafting ad copy, landing pages, email sequences and social posts, then editing them so they do not read like a machine wrote them. This is a skill in itself.
- **Research and analysis.** Pulling a campaign report apart, spotting what moved, and writing the summary your client or manager actually reads.
- **Image and video generation.** Making ad creative without waiting three days for a designer, and knowing the limits.
- **Workflow automation.** Connecting tools so a repetitive task runs on its own. This is where most of the time saving actually lives.
- **Where AI is wrong.** Hallucinated numbers, invented sources, confident nonsense about Indian compliance rules. Students who cannot spot this are dangerous on a client account.
