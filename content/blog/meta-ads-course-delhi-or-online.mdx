A Meta Ads course in Delhi costs between ₹15,000 and ₹40,000 in 2026 and usually runs six to eight weeks. Before you pay for one, the question worth asking is not which city it is in. It is whether you will touch a live ad account with a real budget, because that is the only part of learning Facebook and Instagram ads that cannot be faked.

I say this as someone who has watched students arrive with certificates from well-known places, open Ads Manager, and freeze.

## What a Meta Ads course should cover in 2026

If a syllabus does not include most of this, it is out of date:

- **Account structure.** Campaign, ad set and ad level, and why the wrong structure wastes budget before a single rupee reaches a customer.
- **Objectives and what they actually optimise.** Choosing engagement when you want sales is the most common and most expensive beginner mistake.
- **The Pixel and Conversions API.** With browser tracking as weak as it now is, server-side tracking is no longer advanced material. It is basic hygiene.
- **Creative testing.** How to test a hook, an image and a format without producing noise. Creative is now the biggest lever you have.
- **Audiences in a broad-targeting world.** Meta's algorithm does much of the targeting itself. The skill has shifted from building audiences to feeding the system good creative and clean signals.
- **Budget and bidding.** Campaign budget optimisation, when to scale and when to leave an ad set alone.
- **Reading the numbers.** Cost per result, frequency, return on ad spend, and knowing which one is lying to you.
- **Policy and rejections.** What gets an account restricted and how to recover one.

Anything lighter than that teaches you the buttons, not the job.

## The question that separates good courses from brochures

Ask this, word for word: "Will I run campaigns on a live client ad account with a real budget, and who pays for that budget?"

The answers tell you everything. If it is a dummy account or a simulation, you are buying theory. If you are expected to fund your own ad spend, the course fee is not the real fee, and you should ask what the total comes to. If it is live client accounts supervised by someone who runs them daily, that is the thing worth paying for.

Our students work on live accounts from early in the [Meta Ads Mastery program](/programs/meta-ads-mastery), with budgets our agency side is already spending. The mistakes happen under supervision, which is the entire point.

## Delhi or online, for this subject specifically

Meta Ads is the strongest case for learning online that I know of, and I say that as someone who runs a classroom.

The whole job happens in a browser. There is no lab, no equipment, no physical material. A trainer screen-sharing a live Ads Manager session gives you the same view as leaning over their shoulder, and you can rewatch it, which you cannot do in a room.

What you lose is real but smaller than people assume: the question you ask sideways to the person next to you, and overhearing somebody else's problem, which is often how you learn the thing you did not know to ask about.

For students in Sonipat weighing a Delhi course, the arithmetic rarely favours the commute. Three to four hours a day for eight weeks, to learn a browser-based skill, is a lot of life spent on the Grand Trunk Road. Meta first for visual products, local businesses and faster feedback. Google first for B2B and high-intent searches. Most people end up learning both.

You should not. Ask who funds the ad budget before you enrol. Our students use live client accounts under supervision.
