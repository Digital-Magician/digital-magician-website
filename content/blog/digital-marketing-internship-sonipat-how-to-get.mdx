## Where Sonipat Internships Actually Come From

There isn't one internship market here. There are four separate pools, and most students only look at one of them.

**Local agencies.** Sonipat has a small but growing number of digital marketing agencies and freelancers taking on client work. They're usually short-staffed and will take a capable intern who doesn't need hand-holding.

**Family businesses and shops.** This is the pool nobody thinks of as "an internship," but it's the biggest one. Every clinic, coaching institute, real estate office, and retail shop around Sonipat needs someone to run their Instagram, manage their Google Business Profile, and maybe run a small ad budget. Most owners don't know how to hire for this, so they've never tried. You don't apply here. You pitch.

**Remote internships for NCR startups.** Sonipat is close enough to Delhi, Gurugram, and Noida that you're inside the hiring radius of startups that hire remote interns without caring where you sit. These postings show up on LinkedIn and Internshala, and they're worth applying to alongside the local route, not instead of it.

Students work on live client accounts from our in-house agency, with real budgets, not dummy campaigns. That internship is arranged for you, which is a different starting point than the cold-outreach route below, but the skills you need to use it well are the same either way.

## Why Cold-Applying Fails for Freshers Here

If you're sending your resume to businesses that haven't posted an opening, you're competing with nothing, which sounds like an advantage until you realise nobody's expecting your email either. I've seen dozens of students spend weeks emailing resumes into a void.

The problem isn't the resume. It's what a resume proves. To a Sonipat shop owner or a small agency, a fresher's resume says "I took a course," and that tells them nothing about whether you can help their business. They're not hiring a credential. They're hiring someone to fix a specific, visible problem: an Instagram that hasn't posted in three weeks, Google reviews stuck at 3.2 stars, or a website that loads slowly on a phone.

What works instead is walking in with a small audit of what they're already doing, wrong or missing, and one clear idea for fixing it. This flips the entire conversation. You're no longer asking for a chance. You're showing them a problem they didn't know they had a name for.

## The Script for Approaching a Local Sonipat Business

Here's roughly what I tell students to do, and it takes under an hour to prepare.

Pick a business you already have some connection to, or one within walking distance. Spend twenty minutes looking at their Google Business Profile, Instagram, and website if they have one. Write down three things: what's missing, what's outdated, and one specific fix you'd make first. Then walk in (not call, not message first) and say something close to this:

> "Hi, I'm [name], I'm training in digital marketing here in Sonipat. I checked your Google listing and noticed you don't have photos of [product/service] up, and your last review reply was from four months ago. I can fix both this week for free, and if it helps, I'd like to help manage your Instagram for a month as part of building my portfolio. Would that be alright?"

Notice what's absent: no resume, no rate card, no "please consider me." You're leading with a specific, free fix, and asking for the internship as the natural next step. Most owners say yes to the free fix at minimum, and a good number extend it into an ongoing internship once they see you follow through.

## What to Expect to Be Paid

Set your expectations correctly, because this is where I see the most disappointment.

Locally, Sonipat internships pay somewhere between ₹5,000 and ₹12,000 a month. Family businesses and very small shops sit near the bottom of that range, or offer nothing formal beyond covering your travel. Small agencies and remote NCR startups pay closer to the top, sometimes more if you're already useful on day one.

A meaningful share of these internships, especially the family business ones, are unpaid. That's not automatically a red flag. What matters is whether it's worth your time regardless of the money, and that comes down to one question: are you getting real access?

Unpaid is worth it when you get hands-on control of a live ad account with an actual budget behind it, and you walk out with a case study built on real numbers. It's not worth it if you're mostly doing admin work or scheduling posts someone else wrote. Ask directly before you accept: "Will I have access to the ads account and reporting?" A vague answer is your answer.

## What You Should Actually Get Out of It

The certificate at the end of an internship is close to worthless on its own. What you should be negotiating for, from day one, is two things: access and evidence.

Access means you're the one logging into Google Ads Manager or Meta Business Suite, not watching over someone's shoulder. Evidence means you leave with numbers: cost per lead before and after, follower growth, review count, whatever the business's goal actually was. Screenshot everything as you go, and ask upfront for permission to use anonymised numbers in your portfolio later. Most small business owners are fine with this if you ask before you start.

## Converting an Internship Into a Job

The internship is not the finish line. It's an extended interview, and most students don't treat it that way.

Around week six, if you're delivering results, ask directly whether there's a paid role once the internship ends. Come with numbers, not a request. "Since I started managing your Instagram, you've gone from 40 to 210 followers and DMs are up" lands very differently than "is there a job available." Most small Sonipat businesses that see a real return would rather keep someone who already knows their account than start over with a stranger.

If the business can't offer a role, the case study you built becomes your proof for the next application, local, Delhi NCR, or remote.
