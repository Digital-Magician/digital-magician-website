## Sonipat's Working Professionals Aren't One Group

Over the years running this institute out of our Model Town, Sonipat campus, I've noticed four fairly distinct kinds of working students walk through the door, and each one needs a different slot to actually stick with the course for four months.

**The Delhi or Gurugram commuter.** Leaves the house by 7 or earlier, back well past 7 in the evening most days. Sonipat sends a large number of people down NH-44 and into the Delhi Metro or private cabs every single morning, and for this group, evening batches are close to unworkable. They're either still on the road at 5:30 PM or too drained to focus by 7.

**The local Sonipat employee with fixed hours.** Works a standard 10 to 6 job within the city, maybe in a private company, a bank, or a retail chain. Their commute is short, their hours are predictable, and evenings are genuinely free once they clock out.

**The family business or shop owner.** Runs a shop or a family business, often somewhere along the main market or GT Road belt. Mornings and evenings are peak footfall. But there's a stretch in the early afternoon, usually 12:30 to 2:00 PM, when the shop goes quiet before the evening crowd returns. It's a rhythm specific to how retail and small business work here, and it's genuinely useful if you know to use it.

**The government job holder or teacher.** Could be a school teacher, a clerk, or staff in a government office. Shifts vary, but there's often either a free period mid-morning or a lunch window that lines up with the early afternoon.

## Which Slot Fits Your Situation

| Your situation | Best slot | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Commutes daily to Delhi or Gurugram | 7:30 to 9:00 AM (or weekend catch-up on recordings) | Class finishes before the worst of the commute traffic builds; evenings are unreliable once you're back |
| Works locally in Sonipat, fixed 10 to 6 hours | 5:30 to 7:00 PM | Clocks out at 6, class starts 5:30, and you attend the last half live once you're free, or the full session on shorter days |
| Runs a family business or shop | 12:30 to 2:00 PM | Matches the genuine afternoon lull in footfall before the evening rush returns |
| Government job holder or teacher | 9:00 to 10:30 AM or 12:30 to 2:00 PM | Fits a free period or a lunch window, depending on your specific shift |

None of these are rules carved in stone. A teacher with an early shift might prefer 7:30 AM. A shopkeeper whose spouse can cover the counter might take the 5:30 PM slot instead. The table is a starting point, not a straitjacket, and when students come in for a [free demo class](/free-demo), the first thing we usually do is walk through their actual weekly routine and match it against the real slot, not the "ideal" one on paper.

## The Weekly Time Cost, Honestly

Whichever slot you pick, the course itself doesn't get lighter. Each live session is 90 minutes, five days a week, which comes to 7.5 hours of class. On top of that, plan for another 4 to 6 hours of practice, mock campaigns, keyword research, building your portfolio, most of it happening on weekends or during the quiet stretch of your job if you're a shop owner. That puts most working students at roughly 11.5 to 13.5 hours a week for four months.

That's a real number, and I'd rather you know it upfront than discover it in week two. It's less than a second job, but it's more than a casual hobby course, and treating it like the latter is the fastest way to fall behind.

## Use Your Current Job as Practice, Wherever You Work

Here's the part that's genuinely easier if you're already employed. Every workplace, whether it's a Sonipat shop, a government office, or a Delhi-based company you commute to, has some marketing gap sitting untouched. A Google Business Profile that hasn't been updated in a year. A WhatsApp broadcast list nobody's segmented. A shop's Instagram page that posts once a month if that.

If you're the commuter working in a Gurugram company, offer to help audit their landing pages once you've covered SEO basics in class. If you're the shop owner, your own business is your live lab, run a small test ad during your slower week and track what happens. Teachers and government staff often have the least obvious in-house gap, but even a school's admissions page or a department's social presence is fair practice ground. I go into more of this approach in our [career switch guide for Haryana professionals](/blog/career-switch-digital-marketing-haryana), which covers how switchers turn a current job into a portfolio before they ever apply elsewhere.

## When to Tell Your Employer

Don't lead with "I'm learning this to leave." That framing puts people on edge for no reason, and it's usually not even true yet, you're learning, not resigning. Instead, mention it as upskilling: "I'm picking up digital marketing skills, mind if I try a small improvement on our page or listing." That's an easier conversation, and it often gets you the access to real data and real campaigns that makes the course worth far more.

The one group where I'd say tell your employer earlier rather than later: if your current role already brushes against marketing, even loosely, in retail, admin, or client-facing work. You'll want sign-off to actually touch something real, and asking early avoids friction later.

## The Tiredness Trade-Off, Four Months In

I won't oversell this. Four months of daily classes stacked on a full working day is tiring, no matter which slot you pick. The commuter loses part of an already scarce morning. The shop owner gives up what used to be a breather between customers. The 10 to 6 employee trades their post-work wind-down for ninety more minutes of focus. There will be weeks where the recording, not the live class, is what gets you through, and that's fine, the 12-month replay exists exactly for that.

What I've watched hold true across our 500 plus placed students is that four months of tired evenings or shorter lunches is a small price against years spent in a job that doesn't move. Most people who finish tell me the same thing: the fourth month felt lighter than the first, because by then the course had stopped being extra work and started being the thing that was actually changing their options.
