Back then, most of what got advertised locally was entry-level social media handling for a shop or a coaching centre. Today I'm placing students into performance marketing seats for exporters in the Rai industrial belt, remote analyst roles for Gurugram agencies while they live at home, and in-house marketing jobs for hospitals and schools that never used to budget for a dedicated marketer at all.

## Who's Actually Hiring in and Around Sonipat

Six kinds of employers make up almost the entire local market, and they don't all want the same thing.

**Local marketing and creative agencies.** Small shops, usually two to ten people, running social media and ads for other local businesses. They hire the widest range of skill levels and are often the easiest entry point.

**Manufacturing and export firms in the HSIIDC estate and the Rai industrial belt.** These are B2B businesses selling auto parts, textiles, engineering goods, and similar, and most of them are new to digital marketing entirely. They need LinkedIn presence, a working website, and Google Ads for lead generation, not viral Instagram content.

**Education and coaching institutes.** Competitive exam coaching centres and schools around Sonipat run heavy seasonal ad campaigns around admission cycles and results announcements, and they need someone who can plan a content calendar and run those campaigns on schedule.

**Healthcare clinics and small hospitals.** A lot of local clinics still have no real Google presence. They're hiring for Google Business Profile management, local SEO, and review or reputation handling far more than for anything creative.

**Real estate offices.** Plot sales, resale listings, and small commercial real estate all lean heavily on Meta and Google Ads for lead generation, with CRM follow-up discipline mattering as much as the ad skill itself.

**Retail and D2C brands.** A growing number of Sonipat and Panipat-belt retail businesses are building out their own brand pages and online stores, which is opening up combined content-plus-ads roles that barely existed here three years ago.

## Which Roles Actually Open Up Locally, and Which Need You to Go Remote

Locally, the roles that actually open are fairly generalist. Think "digital marketing executive" covering social media, basic ads, and light SEO together, or a GBP and local SEO handler for a clinic, or an ads executive running admission campaigns for a coaching centre. Employers here rarely have the headcount or budget to hire a narrow specialist.

The more specialised roles, dedicated SEO analyst, growth marketer, marketing automation specialist, are still concentrated in Gurugram, Delhi, and Noida. What's changed is that a real share of those roles now hire remote, and a trained Sonipat candidate is a genuine contender for them in a way that wasn't true a few years ago. ## The Skills Sonipat Employers Actually Ask For

Order matters here, because it tells you what to learn first.

**Meta Ads and Google Ads come first, by a wide margin.** Almost every employer we place students with, whether it's a coaching institute or an export firm, wants proof you can set up, run, and read a live ad campaign. Not describe one in theory, run one.

**GA4 and basic reporting come second.** Employers have gotten sharper about wanting numbers, not vague updates. Can you tell them what a campaign actually delivered, in a report they can read in two minutes?

**Content comes third.** Writing, short-form video direction, and basic design still matter, but on their own they no longer get you hired. Paired with ads and reporting skill, they're what separates an average candidate from someone who gets an above-band offer. ## What's Changed in the Last Two Years

Two shifts stand out from what we're seeing on the ground.

**Remote roles are genuinely open to Sonipat candidates now.** Hybrid and remote hiring normalised across Indian companies after the pandemic, and NCR employers have largely stopped filtering applicants by city pin code the way they used to. India's broader shift toward flexible and remote-friendly hiring has been well documented at a national level too, including by [NASSCOM](https://nasscom.in/) in its coverage of the country's digital workforce growth. Locally, this is the single biggest change we've seen in who our students can realistically apply to.

**AI skills are now asked for by name.** Two years ago, no employer in Sonipat asked about AI tools in an interview. Now it comes up routinely, whether that's using an AI tool to draft ad copy variations, speed up a content calendar, or summarise campaign data. This tracks with the wider trend in digital marketing that outlets like [Think with Google APAC](https://www.thinkwithgoogle.com/intl/en-apac/) have written about, where AI-assisted workflows are becoming a baseline expectation rather than a bonus skill. It's not replacing entry-level roles here yet, but it's raised the floor on what "competent" looks like in an interview.

## Where the Market Is Heading

My honest read: the local employer base is slowly maturing. More Sonipat businesses are budgeting for digital marketing as a real line item rather than handing it to "the nephew who knows Facebook," and that trend has room to keep going, especially among the export and manufacturing firms that are only just starting to invest here. Remote work will keep being the biggest lever for anyone who wants to earn above what a purely local job pays, and I don't see that reversing. AI tools will keep raising the baseline rather than replacing entry roles outright, which means the gap between a candidate who's comfortable with them and one who isn't will keep widening.
