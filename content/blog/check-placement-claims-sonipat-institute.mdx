Before you pay any institute in Sonipat, ask for three things: the written placement policy including its conditions, the placement rate of the last completed batch alongside the batch size, and the phone numbers of three recent students. An institute that actually places people hands all three over without fuss. The reaction to the request tells you more than any brochure.

I run an institute, so read this with appropriate suspicion. I am also the person parents sit in front of after they have paid somewhere else and got nothing, and that conversation is the reason I wrote this down.

## The four questions that do most of the work

**1. Can I read the placement policy in writing, today?**

Not the brochure. The policy document with the conditions in it. If it only exists verbally, there is nothing to hold anyone to later.

**2. What was the placement rate of your last completed batch, and how many students were in it?**

Both numbers, together. "We placed 40 students" means nothing without the batch size. 40 out of 45 is strong. 40 out of 300 is not. Watch for the answer that quietly switches to a lifetime total.

**3. Can I speak to three students who finished in the last six months?**

Not testimonials on a website. Phone numbers. Then actually call them and ask what they earn now, how long the job took to arrive, and what the institute did when it was slow to come.

**4. Who will teach me, and what campaigns are they running right now?**

If the trainer last ran a live campaign in 2021, the syllabus is a museum. Ask what they spent money on last month and what happened.

## Reading the conditions properly

Every genuine guarantee has conditions. That is not a trick, it is the only way a promise like this can mean anything. What matters is whether they are reasonable and written plainly.

| Condition | Fair | Treat with caution |
|---|---|---|
| Attendance | 70% to 80% | 100%, which nobody meets |
| Job applications | A stated number within a stated window | "Sincere effort", undefined |
| Assignments | Complete, with a stated standard | Graded by a scale nobody shows you |
| Refund window | Clear deadline, clear process | "At management discretion" |
| Salary accepted | You must accept a reasonable offer | Any offer, any salary, any city |
| Who decides | Written criteria | A person's opinion, unappealable |

The right-hand column is where the money quietly goes. "Any offer, any city" is the one that catches people: a graduate in Sonipat turns down a ₹9,000 role in another state, and the guarantee is void.

## The claims worth probing

**"100% placement."** Ask what happens to the student who is not placed. There is always one. The answer is the actual policy.

**"Placement partners."** Ask how many students those partners hired last year. A logo on a wall is not a hiring relationship.

**"Average package ₹6 LPA."** Ask for the median and the lowest offer. Averages are dragged upwards by one or two outliers, often someone who was already experienced.

**"Lifetime placement support."** Ask what it consists of after six months. Usually it is access to a WhatsApp group that has gone quiet.

**"Guaranteed internship."** Ask if it is paid, how many hours, and whether it is with a real client or an internal project.

## What matters more than the guarantee

Here is the uncomfortable part. The guarantee is a safety net, and safety nets are not why you pick a course. What gets you hired is what you can show.

In five years of placing students, every offer has turned on the same thing: the candidate could open a laptop and walk an interviewer through campaigns they personally ran. The budget, the decisions, what went wrong, what they changed. The students who cannot do that struggle regardless of which institute's name is on the certificate.

So when you are comparing options in Sonipat, the question underneath all the others is simple: will I finish this course with work I can show, on accounts that were real? If the answer is no, the placement promise is doing a job it cannot actually do.

## A short diligence checklist

Take this with you, to any institute including ours:

- Written placement policy, read before paying, conditions and all.
- Last batch placement rate with batch size.
- Three recent students you can call.
- Trainer's current campaign work.
- Live client accounts, or practice accounts? Who funds the ad budget?
- Total cost: fee, exam charges, tool subscriptions, certification fees.
- Refund policy for leaving early, separate from the placement refund.
- What happens in week five if you fall behind.
- A free demo class, sat through properly, before you commit.

If an institute gets uncomfortable at the third item, you have your answer and you have saved yourself ₹45,000.

## Frequently asked questions

**How do I verify a 100% placement claim?**
Written policy, last batch rate with batch size, and three students you can call. All three, or treat the claim as marketing.

**Assistance or guarantee, what is the difference?**
Assistance means they share openings with no obligation. A guarantee is written, conditional on both sides, and includes a refund if they fail while you met your conditions.

**Is a guarantee with no conditions better?**
No. It is usually unenforceable. Fair, clearly written conditions are a stronger signal.

**What else should I check?**
Who teaches, whether accounts are live, the total cost including extras, and what support looks like in week five.

**Can I talk to your students before enrolling?**
Yes. Ask at the demo and we will give you numbers, not testimonials.
