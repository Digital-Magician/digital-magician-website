A degree in digital marketing takes two to three years and costs ₹1.5 lakh to ₹12 lakh. A certificate course takes three to six months and costs ₹15,000 to ₹60,000. For most private sector marketing jobs, employers check neither first. They check what you have run.

That sounds like a sales pitch from someone who sells certificate courses, so let me be careful and give the degree a fair hearing, because there are situations where it is clearly the better choice.

## Side by side

| | Degree (BBA or MBA with specialisation) | Certificate course |
|---|---|---|
| Duration | 2 to 3 years | 3 to 6 months |
| Cost | ₹1.5 lakh to ₹12 lakh | ₹15,000 to ₹60,000 |
| Teaching | Academic, frameworks, case studies | Applied, platform-first |
| How current | Usually 2 to 3 years behind | Updated as platforms change |
| Live ad budget | Rarely | At the better ones, yes |
| Recognised for government jobs | Yes | No |
| Recognised for higher study | Yes | No |
| Earning while studying | Difficult | Common, many freelance by month three |
| What it proves to a marketing employer | You can finish a long programme | You can run a campaign |

## When the degree is the right answer

**You want a corporate graduate scheme.** Large companies recruit from campuses and filter on qualification before anyone looks at a portfolio. If that is the route you want, the degree is the entry ticket.

**You may want to teach or study further.** A certificate takes you nowhere academically. A degree keeps doors open for a master's, for teaching posts, for research.

**You need a recognised qualification for other reasons.** Government eligibility, a visa application, or simply a family that will not accept a four month course as an education. That last one is not a small thing in Haryana, and I would rather acknowledge it than pretend it does not shape decisions.

**You are 18 and have time.** At 18, three years is a reasonable investment. At 26 with a job to leave, the arithmetic is different.

## When the certificate is the right answer

**You need to be earning soon.** Four months against three years is the entire argument, and for most people it is decisive.

**You already have a degree in something else.** This is our most common student: BA, BCom, BTech, sometimes an MBA, no marketing skill. Adding three more years makes no sense. Adding four months does.

**You want to freelance.** No client in Sonipat has ever asked a freelancer for their degree. They ask to see work and then they ask your rate.

**You want to be current.** The platforms change every quarter. A syllabus approved by a university committee two years ago still teaches Facebook advertising as it worked before the privacy changes, which is a problem when the interview question is about the Conversions API.

## The combination that beats both

If you are in the middle of a degree right now, do not drop it. Do the certificate alongside it.

Second or final year is the right time. You keep the qualification your family wants and your eligibility intact, and you graduate with eighteen months of campaign work behind you. When you apply for your first job, you are competing against classmates whose entire portfolio is a college project.

I have watched this play out at the Rajiv Gandhi Education City campuses. The students who did both were employed before their results came out, usually at ₹5,000 to ₹8,000 a month above their batchmates, because they had something to show on the second question.

Our batches at 7:30 AM and 5:30 PM exist partly for this, so a college timetable and a course can coexist. There is more on how that works day to day in [learning while working part time](/blog/learn-digital-marketing-while-working-part-time).

## What the employer actually checks

In five years of placing students I have watched a lot of interviews. The sequence rarely changes.

They glance at the qualification to see you are not lying. Then they ask what you have run. Then they ask what went wrong and what you did about it. Then, if they are good, they ask you to explain a number on your own dashboard.

A degree survives the first question. Only campaign work survives the fourth. That is the entire difference, and it is why our [digital marketing course in Sonipat](/digital-marketing-course/sonipat) puts students on live client accounts rather than simulations: so there is a fourth answer.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is there a proper degree in digital marketing in India?**
Yes, mostly BBA and MBA specialisations, two to three years, ₹1.5 lakh to ₹12 lakh, academic in style and usually behind the platforms.

**Do employers prefer one?**
For private marketing roles, neither decides it. Portfolio does. Degrees matter for corporate schemes and government eligibility.

**Can I do both?**
Yes, and it is the strongest combination. Do the course during your second or final year.

**Is the degree worth the money?**
If you want to teach, enter a corporate track, or need the qualification for other reasons, yes. If you want to be employable quickly, it buys time you could spend earning.

**What about an MBA later?**
Work first. An MBA is worth far more after two or three years of real marketing experience than straight after a BBA, and your employer may pay for it.
