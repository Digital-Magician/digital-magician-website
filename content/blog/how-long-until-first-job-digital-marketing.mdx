For our graduates, about 80% are placed within 30 days of finishing. Most of those offers arrive during the two month internship rather than after it.

The other 20% take two to four months, and after five years of watching this, the reasons are boringly consistent. They are not about talent. They are about when you started applying, what you can show, and how narrowly you drew the map.

## The realistic timeline, week by week

This assumes a four month course with a two month internship after it, and a student who starts applying while still studying.

| Stage | What happens | Typical timing |
|---|---|---|
| Month 3 of the course | First applications go out, portfolio has two live campaigns | Week 10 to 12 |
| First interviews | Screening calls, one or two assignments | Week 12 to 16 |
| Internship begins | Real accounts, references start forming | Week 17 |
| First offer | Often from the internship firm or a placement drive | Week 18 to 24 |
| Placed | Joining date agreed | Week 20 to 26 |

Count from the first day of the course and the common outcome is a job around month five or six, with the offer landing before the internship ends.

Now read the same table for someone who waits until the course finishes to start looking. Everything shifts right by ten weeks, and they are also applying without the internship on their CV. That single decision is the difference between a one month wait and a four month one.

## What the 20% have in common

I pulled this from our own records rather than from a brochure, and it is uncomfortable reading in places.

**They started late.** By far the biggest factor. Applications sent in month three convert much better than the same applications sent in month five, because hiring takes three to six weeks and you want that clock running while you still have the structure of a course around you.

**They had coursework, not accounts.** A fresher with three live campaigns and real numbers interviews completely differently from one with a certificate and a practice assignment. The [portfolio question](/blog/how-to-build-digital-marketing-portfolio) is the single biggest lever a student controls.

**They applied to one city only.** Usually Sonipat, sometimes only Delhi. Sonipat has a genuine but limited number of openings in any given month. Adding Kundli, Rai, Panipat, remote roles and Delhi NCR multiplies the pool several times over.

**They turned down the first offer.** Often sensibly. But a ₹18,000 offer refused in week 20 can mean a ₹22,000 offer accepted in week 34, and the lost fourteen weeks of experience usually cost more than the ₹4,000 gained.

**A small number had something else happening.** Exams, family obligations, a health problem. That is life, and it is not a failure of the course or the student.

## What shortens the wait, in order of effect

Four things, and they are all available to you from week one.

**Apply from month three.** Not after. There is no advantage to waiting until you feel ready, because nobody feels ready and the process is slow anyway.

**Get real accounts on your CV.** If your course does not put you on live client work, find your own. A friend's gym, a relative's shop, anything with a real ₹3,000 budget and a real outcome. One real account beats six certificates.

**Widen the geography before you widen the salary band.** Remote roles with Bangalore and Mumbai agencies have changed this considerably since 2024, and they pay closer to metro rates.

**Treat the internship as the interview.** Most of our graduates are hired by the firm they interned with, or by someone that firm referred them to. People who treat the internship as a formality to be completed lose that advantage entirely. ## About institutes that promise a date

Be careful with any specific promise about timing.

"Placed within 30 days" as an average is a measurable claim and reasonable to make. "Guaranteed job in 30 days" is not, because no institute controls when a company opens a vacancy. Anyone offering a date rather than a range is either guessing or counting something you would not count as a job.

The questions worth asking are simple: what proportion were placed, in what window, counted how, and what are the conditions on your guarantee. If the answers are vague, the number is decorative. We wrote the full list of checks in [how to check an institute's placement claims](/blog/check-placement-claims-sonipat-institute).

## What to do in the waiting weeks

The period between applying and joining is where most people lose momentum, and it is also the cheapest time to improve your position. Four weeks of the right activity here changes which offers you get.

**Keep one account running.** Even a tiny one. A friend's business with a ₹2,000 monthly budget keeps your CV current and gives you something new to say in each interview. Candidates who went quiet for two months have to explain the gap.

**Write up each interview the same evening.** What they asked, what you fumbled, what you will say next time. By the fourth interview you are noticeably better, and the improvement is deliberate rather than accidental.

**Fix the one thing that keeps coming up.** If three interviewers asked about Google Analytics and you were weak each time, that is the week's work. Most freshers have exactly one recurring gap and ignore it.

**Apply on Tuesday and Wednesday mornings.** A small effect but a real one. Applications sent on Friday evening sit behind sixty others by Monday.

## The things that get people rejected

Worth naming, because they are fixable and most of them are not about skill.

Applying with a CV listing tools instead of outcomes. "Google Ads, Meta Ads, SEO, Canva" tells an employer nothing. "Ran ₹60,000 of Google Ads across three local accounts, brought cost per enquiry from ₹310 to ₹180" tells them everything.

Not being able to explain a number from your own portfolio. If a campaign is on your CV, know what it spent and what it returned. Being caught out here ends interviews quickly.

No digital footprint at all. An employer will search your name. A LinkedIn profile with your campaign work on it is a low effort, high return piece of preparation, and a surprising number of candidates skip it.

Asking about salary in the first five minutes. Reasonable in principle, costly in practice at fresher level.

And the quiet one: being unreachable. Missed calls not returned the same day, an email address checked twice a week. Local firms in Sonipat and Kundli hire fast and informally, and they move on to the next candidate quickly.

## If you are past three months and nothing has landed

This happens, and panicking is the wrong response. Work through four things in order.

Check whether your applications are reaching anyone. Thirty applications on one job portal is not thirty applications; it is one channel. Add direct emails to local firms, LinkedIn messages to marketing managers, and walk-ins in your own city, which still work remarkably well in Sonipat.

Check your CV against the outcome test above. Half the problem is usually here.

Check your geography and your floor. If you have ruled out everything under ₹25,000 and everywhere outside one city, you may have ruled out the whole market available to you this quarter.

Then ask for a review from someone who hires. Not a friend, someone who actually reads CVs. Fifteen minutes with that person is worth more than another hundred applications. Our students get this from the placement team, and anyone else should ask a marketing manager they know; most will say yes if asked plainly. ## Frequently asked questions

**How long is the wait?**
Around 80% of our graduates are placed within 30 days of finishing, with most offers arriving during the internship.

**Why do some take four months?**
They started applying late, had no live accounts to show, limited themselves to one city, or declined the first offer.

**Should I accept a low first offer?**
Usually yes, if it includes real accounts and a senior who reviews your work. Experience compounds faster than a ₹4,000 difference.

**When do I start applying?**
Month three of a four month course. Hiring takes three to six weeks, so start the clock early.

**Does a certificate shorten the wait?**
Barely. A live campaign with real numbers shortens it a lot. The certificate gets you past a filter at best.
