For a manufacturer or trading firm in the Kundli and Rai belt, training two of your own people usually costs less than four months of an agency retainer, and the knowledge stays in the building when staff change.

It only works under one condition, and I would rather say it at the top than at the bottom: somebody in your firm has to own the work afterwards. Training does not create that person. It equips one who already exists.

![A trainer explaining a marketing dashboard to three staff members in a small factory office near Sonipat](/blog-images/corporate-digital-marketing-training-sonipat-kundli.jpg)

## Why firms here keep asking about this

The industrial belt north of Sonipat has a specific problem. There are hundreds of firms doing between two and forty crores a year who sell to other businesses, or to dealers, or increasingly to customers directly. They know they are losing enquiries to competitors who show up on Google and Instagram. They have usually tried one of two things.

The first is hiring an agency, often from Delhi, at ₹15,000 to ₹40,000 a month. Some of those engagements work well. Many end after five months with nobody in the firm able to explain what was done or what it returned.

The second is handing the job to whoever in the office is youngest. That person makes a Facebook page, posts for three weeks, and stops, because nobody told them what success looks like or gave them a budget.

Training is the third option, and for a firm with a willing in-house person it is usually the cheapest of the three.

| Train two staff, two week engagement | ₹35,000 plus your ad spend | The above, plus accounts and reporting already built |
| Agency retainer | ₹1,80,000 to ₹4,80,000 plus ad spend | Whatever they hand over, which is often less than you expect |
| Hire a fresher marketer | ₹2,40,000 to ₹3,60,000 salary | A full time person, if you can keep them |

Those first-year numbers are not the whole picture. An agency brings experience you cannot buy in four days, and a good one is worth paying. But if your monthly ad budget is ₹20,000, paying ₹25,000 to manage it is the wrong shape, and most firms in this belt are in exactly that position.

## Who to send, and who not to

This is the part that decides whether the money is wasted.

**Send the person who will do the work.** Usually a sales coordinator, a marketing executive, or a family member in the business. Someone who will be at a desk on a Tuesday actually writing the ad.

**Send one person who signs off on money.** The owner, a director, a general manager. Four days is enough for them to understand what they are approving and to stop asking for the wrong things.

**Do not send only juniors.** They will learn the work and then wait three weeks for permission to spend ₹500.

**Do not send only the owner.** They will understand it, agree with it, and then be on a site visit when the campaign needs attention.

The pairing is the point. In our experience, firms that send a doer and a decider are still running their own campaigns a year later. Firms that send one or the other usually are not.

## What four days actually covers

Not a syllabus. A working set of campaigns by the end of it.

- **Day one.** Where your enquiries currently come from, with the actual numbers pulled from your own accounts. Most firms find at least one surprise here, often that a channel they ignore is producing more than one they pay for.
- **Day two.** Google: the search terms your buyers use, a live search campaign built with your budget, and your Google Business Profile fixed properly. For a local manufacturer this single day often pays for the programme.
- **Day three.** Meta ads and WhatsApp. Dealer and B2B targeting, lead forms that do not fill with rubbish, and a reply flow your sales team will actually use. The [WhatsApp marketing guide](/blog/whatsapp-marketing-guide-indian-businesses) covers the ground we work through here.
- **Day four.** Measurement and handover. A weekly report your team can produce in fifteen minutes, who checks what, and what to do when a campaign stops working.

Everything is built on your own accounts, with your own logins, on your own budget. Nothing is a demo account. When we leave, the campaigns are running and they belong to you.

## When I would tell you not to do this

I would rather lose the booking than take money for something that will not work.

Do not train your staff if nobody has four to six hours a week to spare. Marketing done for three weeks and then abandoned is worse than not starting, because you learn nothing and still spend the money.

Do not train if your product genuinely sells only through long relationship selling with twelve buyers in the country. Your money belongs in travel and trade shows, not in ads.

And do not train if you are hoping to replace a person you cannot keep. A firm that loses its marketing executive every nine months has a retention problem, and training the next one will not fix it. ## Three firms, three different answers

Abstract advice is less useful than examples, so here are three real shapes of enquiry we get from this belt, with what we actually recommended. Details are changed, the decisions are not.

**A fastener manufacturer in Kundli, forty staff, selling to dealers across North India.** Their enquiries came entirely from two salesmen and a twelve year old relationship book. They wanted Instagram. We trained their sales coordinator on Google search ads and the Business Profile instead, because dealers looking for a supplier search on Google and do not scroll Instagram at 11 AM on a Tuesday. Four months later their enquiry volume had roughly doubled off a ₹14,000 monthly budget, and the coordinator runs it in about three hours a week.

**A furniture showroom on the Delhi bypass, family run.** Here the answer was not training at all. They had a Business Profile with the wrong timings, no photographs taken since 2022, and 41 unanswered reviews. We fixed those in a single afternoon session, showed the owner's son how to post weekly, and told them to come back in six months. The free work moved more revenue than a programme would have.

**A packaging firm in Rai with a marketing executive already in post.** This was the ideal case. One capable person, a real budget, and an owner who attended all four days. They now run their own Google and Meta campaigns and brought in an agency only for their website rebuild, which is sensible division of labour.

The pattern is worth noticing. Two of those three did not need a full programme. A firm that tells you every problem requires their biggest package is not giving you advice, it is giving you a quotation.

## What goes wrong after the training

Firms that stop running campaigns usually stop for one of four reasons, and all four are predictable enough to plan against.

**The trained person leaves.** This is why we insist on two attendees. One person trained is a single point of failure, and in this belt people do move.

**Nobody protects the time.** Marketing is the first thing dropped when a shipment is delayed. If the owner does not treat the three hours a week as fixed, it disappears by month two.

**The budget gets cut after one bad month.** Every account has a bad month. Cutting spend in response usually makes the next month worse, and then the conclusion is that digital marketing does not work here.

**Nobody is answering the enquiries.** The commonest failure of all, and the most painful to watch. Campaigns generate thirty WhatsApp enquiries and the sales team replies to eleven of them, two days later. Fix the reply process before you spend anything on ads.

We cover all four on day four, and we check back at thirty and ninety days, because a programme that is forgotten by month three has wasted your money regardless of how good the four days were.
