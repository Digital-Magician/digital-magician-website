If you're asking whether digital marketing is a good career for your child, here is the honest answer: yes, it is a real and growing career, but it isn't a government job, and I won't pretend it is. It comes with real income potential, no pension, growing demand, and no guaranteed permanence. I'd rather tell you that plainly now than have you discover it later.

I'm Gaurav Malik, founder of Digital Magician in Sonipat. Most of my conversations aren't with the student who wants to enrol. They're with the parent sitting next to them, often quieter, often more worried, usually the one paying the fee. You've seen a government exam form, an engineering seat, or an MBA brochure make sense on paper. A "digital marketing course" sounds newer, less tested, and harder to judge. That worry is fair. Let me walk through it the way I would if you were sitting across my desk at our Model Town campus.

## Is This a Real Career, or Just a Trend?

It's real, and the evidence is around you, not in a brochure. Every shop, clinic, coaching centre and factory in Sonipat that wants customers online now needs someone who can run a Google Ads campaign, manage an Instagram page, or get a website to show up in search. That need didn't exist twenty years ago. It exists in nearly every business today, and it keeps growing as more of India's commerce moves online. India's internet and e-commerce base has expanded for over a decade straight, and [industry data from IBEF](https://www.ibef.org/industry/ecommerce) shows no sign of that curve flattening. That's not a fad. A fad fades. This has only grown.

Our own [digital marketing course in Sonipat](/digital-marketing-course/sonipat) exists because that demand is local, not hypothetical. We've placed 500 plus students from Sonipat and nearby towns into real marketing roles, not because the field is trendy, but because businesses are actively hiring for it. If you want the fuller picture on where this career stands in India right now, I've written a longer piece on [whether digital marketing is a good career in India](/blog/is-digital-marketing-a-good-career-india) that goes into the data.

## What About Job Security, Compared to a Government Job?

I won't compare this to a sarkari naukri and pretend they're the same, because they aren't. A government job offers a pension, fixed hours, and a kind of permanence that private sector work simply doesn't match. Digital marketing offers none of that.

What it offers instead is a portable skill. If your child learns to run ads and manage SEO well, that skill travels with them, to a Gurugram agency, a Delhi startup, a client in another state, or a freelance income built from home. A government exam has a limited number of seats and no guarantee of clearing it after years of preparation. A skill, once learned properly, keeps earning regardless of who's hiring this month. Neither path is risk-free. The risks are just different, and your child (and you) should choose with eyes open, not with false comfort in either direction.

## What Will My Child Actually Earn, and When?

Most freshers we place step into roles in the three to five lakh per annum range within weeks of finishing the course, not years. That's not everyone, and I won't inflate the number to sound impressive. It depends on effort, the portfolio your child builds during training, and how well they interview. But it's a realistic, verifiable range, and it's usually faster than the timeline for a degree-only candidate with no practical skill to show.

Earnings grow from there with experience, the same as any skilled profession. A student who freelances alongside a job or builds a strong track record in Google Ads or SEO within two to three years often earns considerably more than that starting figure. What matters most in the first year isn't the exact number. It's whether your child is actually working, building real experience, instead of sending out resumes with nothing to show.

## Does My Child Still Need a Degree?

Yes. I say this even though it would be easier for my business if I said no. Do both. A degree gives your child a credential that still matters for many employers, a general education, and options that stay open if their interests change later. A digital marketing course gives them a practical skill that a degree alone doesn't teach.

We recommend our course alongside a BA, B.Com, BBA or any degree, not instead of one. Most of our students study and train at the same time, or do the course right after graduating. I've written more on this exact comparison in our piece on a [digital marketing course vs an MBA degree](/blog/digital-marketing-course-vs-mba-degree), which covers when a further degree is worth it and when a focused skill gets your child working faster.

## Is It Safe for My Daughter to Work in This Field?

This question comes up in nearly every parent conversation I have, and I take it seriously rather than brushing past it. Yes, it's safe, and it's genuinely one of the strengths of this field for daughters specifically. A large share of digital marketing work, managing ad accounts, writing content, running SEO, reporting on campaign numbers, can be done remotely or on flexible hours. Many of our women students manage clients from home, on their own schedule, without needing to commute alone late at night or relocate to another city. That flexibility isn't a side benefit we add on. It's built into how this work actually functions.
