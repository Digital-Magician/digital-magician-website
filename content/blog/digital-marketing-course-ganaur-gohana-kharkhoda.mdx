Ganaur is the easy case. At 18km and 20 to 25 minutes on NH44, it's a genuinely commutable distance, not a technically-possible-but-brutal one. I have students who ride in from Ganaur on scooters for the 9:00 AM or 12:30 PM slot and it barely disrupts their day. If you're from Ganaur, my honest recommendation is offline. You get the classroom energy, you can grab me or another trainer between sessions with a quick question, and the ride itself is short enough that it won't wear you down by week six the way a longer commute does. Gohana is where I stop being diplomatic. It's roughly 45km from our campus, and in real traffic that's an hour or more each way. I've watched a handful of Gohana students try it in the early weeks, energetic and determined, and burn out by month two. Not because the syllabus was hard. Because the road was.

## Kharkhoda: It Depends on Your Bus, Not Just the Distance

Kharkhoda sits at around 25km, which on paper looks similar to Ganaur. In practice, it's a different conversation, because the limiting factor isn't distance, it's the bus connection. If a bus runs reliably to Sonipat around your preferred slot, offline works fine and travel time lands around 35 to 40 minutes. If the bus schedule doesn't line up, or you're relying on a connection that gets unpredictable during peak hours, you can end up spending 50 minutes or more each way, and that unpredictability is worse than the distance itself.

My honest advice for Kharkhoda students: check your actual bus timing against our four slots before deciding. If it fits cleanly, come offline, it's a workable commute. If it doesn't, or if you'd rather not build your schedule around a bus that sometimes runs late, the live online batch removes that variable completely.
