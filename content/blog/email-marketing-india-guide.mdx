Yes, email marketing still works in 2026, and it remains one of the highest-ROI channels in digital marketing anywhere in the world. In India though, it doesn't work the way it did five years ago. WhatsApp has taken over the fast, local, "hi is this available" layer of communication, and email has moved into a different job: closing leads, retaining customers, and running the automated flows that a WhatsApp broadcast can't. If you're considering an email marketing course right now, you're not learning a channel on its way out. You're learning the backbone of B2B lead nurturing, e-commerce recovery, and edtech onboarding at nearly every serious Indian business I work with.

I say this having run email flows for our own agency clients and taught the module inside our four-month course to hundreds of students. The pattern is consistent. Businesses that treat email as an afterthought leave money sitting in abandoned carts and cold leads. Businesses that build even one solid welcome sequence see it pay for itself within the first month.

Industry trackers back this up. [HubSpot's research on email marketing statistics](https://blog.hubspot.com/marketing/email-marketing-stats) puts average returns between $36 and $42 for every $1 spent, consistently ahead of most other digital channels. The reason is simple: email costs almost nothing to send at scale, and it reaches people who already opted in.

## Where Email Still Wins in India

**B2B lead nurturing.** A business buyer rarely says yes on the first WhatsApp message. They want a case study, a proposal, a follow-up two weeks later when the budget clears. Email is where that sequence lives, because it's expected to be formal, it's easy to forward to a decision maker, and it doesn't feel like the pushy follow-up a WhatsApp ping can feel like.

**E-commerce, especially abandoned carts and offers.** Roughly 7 in 10 online carts in India get abandoned before checkout. An automated abandoned-cart email, sent two to three hours after someone leaves, is one of the few marketing messages that regularly converts at double digit rates because the intent was already there. Festival and flash-sale offers work the same way: email lets you segment by past purchase instead of blasting everyone.

**Newsletters.** A weekly or monthly newsletter builds the kind of long-term trust a WhatsApp broadcast can't, mostly because people expect to skim it rather than reply instantly. For a coaching business, a D2C brand, or a service provider, a newsletter keeps you top of mind between purchases without feeling like a sales pitch every time.

**Edtech nurture sequences.** This is close to home for us. A student who fills a form for a free demo but doesn't join immediately needs three or four touchpoints before they commit, syllabus details, a fee breakdown, a placement story. Email carries that sequence better than a single WhatsApp message because the student can read it again before deciding.

## Where WhatsApp Beats Email

WhatsApp wins on speed and local retail. If a customer in Sonipat wants to know whether a shop has stock right now, or wants a quick price check before walking in, WhatsApp answers in seconds. Read rates on WhatsApp are also far higher than email for time-sensitive updates like order confirmations or delivery status.

Businesses that get this right don't pick one channel. They use WhatsApp to warm someone up, then use email to close the sale and keep the relationship going. ## The Core Skills Employers Actually Want

An email marketing course that only teaches you how to design a nice-looking email is incomplete. What gets you hired is the system behind it:

- **List building with lead magnets.** A free checklist, a discount code, or a mini-guide in exchange for an email address, built to attract the right kind of subscriber, not just any subscriber.
- **Segmentation.** Splitting a list by behaviour (opened but didn't click, bought once, abandoned a cart) instead of sending the same email to everyone.
- **Automation flows.** Welcome sequences, abandoned-cart recovery, and re-engagement campaigns that run on triggers, not manual sends.
- **Subject lines that get opened.** Short, specific, and honest about what's inside. Curiosity works, but a misleading subject line kills trust fast.
- **Deliverability basics.** SPF and DKIM are the authentication records that tell inbox providers your email is genuinely from you, and getting them wrong is the single biggest reason good emails land in spam.
- **Avoiding the spam folder.** Clean lists, a real sending pattern, and content that doesn't trip spam filters with too many links or shouty subject lines.

## Tools You Should Actually Learn On

You don't need an expensive tool to learn this well. Three platforms cover almost everything an Indian small business or agency uses, and all three have usable free tiers:

- **Mailchimp**, the most beginner-friendly, with a drag-and-drop builder that's easy to pick up in a weekend.
- **Brevo** (formerly Sendinblue), whose free plan includes automation workflows that most competitors lock behind a paid tier.
- **Zoho Campaigns**, useful because it connects directly into Zoho's CRM, which is closer to how mid-size Indian companies actually structure their marketing stack.

Learn one deeply rather than three shallowly. The logic (lists, segments, triggers, templates) carries over between tools once you understand it in one.

## Metrics That Actually Matter Now

Open rate used to be the go-to number. It still matters, but be honest about its limits. Since Apple's Mail Privacy Protection started pre-loading images on iPhone Mail, a chunk of "opens" are automated, not a real person reading your email. Treat open rate as a rough signal, not gospel.

Click rate is more reliable because it needs an actual click. And conversions, whether that's a purchase, a form fill, or a demo booking, are what actually tell you if the email worked. A campaign with a mediocre open rate but a strong conversion rate is doing its job. Don't optimise for the vanity number.

## A Starter Automation Blueprint

The fastest way to learn this is to build one flow and watch it run. Here's the blueprint I give students building their first automation, plus the two flows that come right after it.

| Flow | Trigger | Goal | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| Welcome series | New signup or lead magnet download | Build trust, introduce the brand | Email 1 (instant): deliver the promised freebie. Email 2 (day 2): share a quick win or case study. Email 3 (day 5): soft offer or next step |
| Abandoned cart | Cart left for 2+ hours without checkout | Recover the sale | A reminder email with the exact items, followed by a small incentive if there's no action in 24 hours |
| Re-engagement | No opens or clicks in 60-90 days | Win back or clean the list | "Still want to hear from us?" email with a clear opt-out, so inactive contacts stop dragging down deliverability |

That three-email welcome flow above is usually the very first automation I have a student build, because it's short enough to finish in a single class and it teaches triggers, timing, and segmentation all at once.
