## Why a Technical Degree Plus Marketing Skills Works

Here's something I tell every DCRUST student who walks into our Sonipat campus. You already have half the skillset a good marketer needs. You just don't know it yet.

BTech students are trained to think in systems: inputs, processes, outputs, iteration. That's exactly how performance marketing works. A Google Ads campaign is a feedback loop. You set an input (budget, targeting, creative), measure an output (clicks, conversions, cost per lead), and iterate. Students from a data structures background pick up campaign optimization faster than most, because they're already used to thinking in variables and outcomes rather than gut feeling.

What's actually missing isn't technical skill. It's commercial thinking, the instinct for what a business cares about. An engineer might build a perfectly functional landing page and never ask whether it converts visitors into leads. A marketer asks that first. Teaching a DCRUST student to run an ad account or set up analytics is quick, usually weeks. Teaching them to ask "what does this cost us and what do we get back" takes longer, and it's the piece that separates a good marketing hire from someone who just knows the tools.

This also makes engineering plus marketing a rarer, more valuable combination. Most marketing graduates understand the commercial side but struggle with anything technical, tag management, automation, ad platform APIs. A DCRUST student who adds marketing skills gets comfortable on both sides of that line, and martech roles specifically look for people who can talk to both a CMO and a developer.
