A digital marketing agency in Sonipat will charge you somewhere between ₹8,000 and ₹40,000 a month, before ad spend. Learning to run the campaigns yourself costs ₹45,000 once. Which one makes sense comes down to a single question that has nothing to do with money: how many hours a week can you actually give it?

I run both sides of this. Our agency work funds the institute, and I have sat on both sides of that invoice, so this is as straight as I can make it.

| Monthly fee | ₹8,000 to ₹40,000 | Nothing after the course |
| Ad budget | Separate, you pay it | Separate, you pay it |

The first year arithmetic looks decisive, and for a business with time it usually is. But the hours row is the one that decides outcomes, and people consistently lie to themselves about it.

## When hiring an agency is the right call

Be honest about which of these you are.

**Your time is worth more than the fee.** If you run a clinic seeing 40 patients a day, four hours a week on ad accounts is not a saving, it is a loss. Pay someone.

**You need results this month.** Training takes four months before you are competent. A seasonal business cannot wait for that.

**The work is genuinely specialised.** E-commerce feeds, multi-city campaigns, anything with more than a couple of offers running at once. That is not a weekend skill.

**You have tried and stopped.** If you already took a course and the account has been untouched for three months, that is useful information about your week, not about your ability.

## When learning it yourself is the right call

**One business, one or two offers.** A boutique, a gym, a coaching centre, a dental clinic. The campaign structure is simple enough that the work is mostly consistency.

**Your margins cannot carry a retainer.** A ₹15,000 monthly fee against ₹60,000 of monthly profit is a serious tax. Learning once is cheaper.

**You want to stop being dependent.** The most common reason business owners sit in our batches is not the money. It is that they could not tell whether their agency was doing anything, and they were tired of that feeling.

**Someone in the family can own it.** A son, daughter or cousin who does the marketing is the most common arrangement in Sonipat businesses, and it works better than either extreme.

## The in-between option nobody mentions

You do not have to pick a side. The arrangement I most often recommend to Sonipat business owners is this: learn enough to supervise, then keep a specialist for execution.

Three months in, you will be able to open the account, read the numbers, ask the right three questions and know when an answer is nonsense. You may still pay someone to run it day to day. The difference is that you are now a client who cannot be comfortably misled, and that changes the quality of work you receive. It changes the invoice too.

## What to ask any agency before signing

Ask these five and listen to the shape of the answers, not just the content.

1. **Do I get owner access to my own Google and Meta accounts?** The only correct answer is yes. These accounts are your property.
2. **What exactly is fee and what is ad spend?** You would be surprised how often that line is deliberately blurred.
3. **What does success look like in month one, and how will you report it?** Vague answers here predict vague reporting later.
4. **Who specifically works on my account, and what else do they run?** A junior with 25 accounts cannot give yours attention.
5. **What happens if I leave? Do I keep the account, the creatives, the data?** Get this answered before you start, not after.

An agency that answers all five plainly is probably worth the money. The checklist in [how to check placement claims](/blog/check-placement-claims-sonipat-institute) uses the same logic applied to institutes, including mine.

We gave the owner's nephew the ads part of the course. Within six weeks he had taken over the accounts. It worked because the nephew had four hours a week. That is the whole variable.

## Frequently asked questions

**What does an agency in Sonipat charge?**
₹8,000 to ₹40,000 a month in fees, plus ad spend. Always separate the two before comparing quotes.

**Is learning cheaper than hiring?**
Over a year, nearly always. A four month course costs roughly three months of a mid-range retainer.

**Can I really run my own ads?**
For one local business with a couple of offers, yes. Consistency defeats people, not complexity.

**How do I judge my current agency?**
Get owner access to your accounts and look at spend, enquiries and cost per enquiry. Resistance to giving access is itself the answer.

**What if I learn and then do not have time?**
Then you hire, but as an informed client. That is still worth the course fee.
