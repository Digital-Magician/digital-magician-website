Digital marketing interviews for freshers test two things: whether you know the fundamentals (SEO, SEM, CTR, CPC, ROAS) and whether you've actually run a campaign, even a small one. Below are the questions that come up most often, grouped by round, with a short model answer approach for each. No essays, just the shape of a good answer.

I sit on the hiring side too. Agencies call me looking for candidates, and I've watched hundreds of these interviews happen from both chairs, as the person asking and the person coaching the candidate beforehand. The pattern is consistent. Candidates who fumble know the definitions but have never opened Ads Manager. Candidates who get offers can point to a real number and say "here's what I did to get it."

## HR and Basics Round

This round checks fit and communication, not technical depth. Keep answers short and specific.

### 1. Tell me about yourself for a marketing role

Skip the life story. Give your background in one sentence, then pivot straight to why you're suited to marketing: a project you ran, a certification you finished, or a skill you built. End with what you're looking for in this role. Thirty to forty-five seconds, not three minutes.

### 2. Why digital marketing?

Avoid the generic "I like creativity and technology" line. Interviewers hear it fifty times a week. Instead, name a specific moment: a campaign you noticed working on you as a consumer, a small business you helped, a course project that clicked. Specificity is what separates you from the next candidate.

### 3. Why should we hire you over another fresher?

This is your chance to mention proof, not enthusiasm. If you've run a real campaign or built a mock one with numbers, this is where it goes. "I've managed a live campaign" beats "I'm a fast learner" every time.

### 4. What do you know about our company?

Spend ten minutes before the interview on their website, their social channels, and their recent ads (check the Meta Ads Library or Google's transparency report). Mention one specific thing you noticed. It shows you did the work, and most freshers don't.

### 5. Where do you see yourself in two years?

Employers aren't testing ambition here. They're testing whether you'll stick around long enough to be worth training. Say you want to go deeper into one channel (ads, SEO, or content) and take on more ownership. Avoid saying you plan to start your own agency.

## Concept Round

This round checks whether you actually understand the fundamentals, not whether you've memorised definitions.

### 6. SEO vs SEM: what's the difference?

SEO is earning traffic through organic search rankings by improving content and site structure. SEM is buying visibility through paid search ads, usually on Google Ads. Both aim to show up when someone searches, but one takes time and costs nothing per click, the other is instant and costs money per click. Mention that most real strategies use both together.

### 7. What is CTR, CPC, and ROAS?

CTR (click-through rate) is the percentage of people who see your ad and click it. Google's own definition puts it simply as [clicks divided by impressions](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/2615875), and it feeds directly into your Ad Rank. CPC (cost per click) is what you pay each time someone clicks. ROAS (return on ad spend) is the revenue you earn for every rupee spent. Give the formula for each in one line, and mention how CTR ties into Ad Rank so the interviewer sees you understand the connection, not just the definitions.

### 8. How does the Google Ads auction work?

Explain that it's not simply the highest bidder who wins. Google calculates an Ad Rank using your bid multiplied by Quality Score (which covers expected CTR, ad relevance, and landing page experience). A lower bid with a strong Quality Score can beat a higher bid with a weak one. That last point is what most freshers miss, so say it clearly.

### 9. Organic vs paid: what's the difference?

Organic results appear because of relevance and site authority, and you don't pay per visit. Paid results appear because you're bidding for that placement, and you pay per click or impression. Add that organic builds slower but compounds over time, while paid gives you control and speed but stops the moment you stop paying.

### 10. What is remarketing?

Remarketing (also called retargeting) means showing ads to people who already visited your site or app but didn't convert. It works because most visitors don't buy on the first visit, so you stay visible while they decide. Mention a real example: someone browses a product, leaves, then sees that same product in an ad the next day.

### 11. What's the difference between reach and impressions?

Reach is the number of unique people who saw your ad. Impressions is the total number of times it was shown, including repeat views to the same person. One person seeing an ad three times counts as one reach but three impressions. This trips up a lot of candidates, so having it clean is worth the effort.

### 12. What is a conversion rate?

The percentage of people who complete a desired action (a purchase, a form fill, a sign-up) out of everyone who visited or clicked. It's calculated as conversions divided by total clicks or visits, multiplied by 100. Tie it back to why marketers obsess over it: traffic without conversions doesn't pay anyone's salary.

## Practical Round

This round is where a portfolio does most of the talking for you. If you've run even a small campaign, this section becomes easy.

### 13. How would you spend ₹10,000 a month for a local business?

Don't just name platforms. Walk through a split: for example, a chunk on Google Search Ads for people actively searching, a chunk on Meta Ads for local awareness and retargeting, and a small reserve for testing creatives. Explain your reasoning for the split based on the business type, not a fixed formula you memorised.

### 14. Walk me through a campaign you ran

This is the question a portfolio wins outright. State the goal, the budget, what you set up (targeting, creative, tracking), what happened, and what you'd change next time. Even a ₹500 test campaign on your own account works if you can speak to real numbers. Vague answers about "learning a lot" don't land here.

### 15. How do you measure success for a campaign?

Success depends on the goal, so say that first. For a sales campaign, it's ROAS and cost per acquisition. For a brand campaign, it's reach and engagement. For a lead-gen campaign, it's cost per lead and lead quality. Naming the right metric for the right goal is what separates someone who's studied marketing from someone who's only read about it.

## Round-by-Round Cheat Sheet

| Round | What They Test | How to Prepare |
|---|---|---|
| HR and basics | Communication, fit, motivation | Practice a 30-second intro; research the company beforehand |
| Concept round | Whether you understand fundamentals, not just terms | Explain each definition out loud in your own words, with a real example |
| Practical round | Whether you've actually done the work | Run one small real or mock campaign and be ready to walk through the numbers |
