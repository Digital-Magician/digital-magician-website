A free demo class is the cheapest research you will ever do on a fifty thousand rupee decision, and most people waste it by sitting quietly at the back.

Bring these twelve questions written on paper. Ask them out loud. How an institute answers them, and how comfortable they are being asked, tells you more than any brochure, review or AI summary.

![An instructor showing a laptop screen to two visiting students and a parent at a small institute in Sonipat](/blog-images/questions-to-ask-demo-class-before-paying.jpg)

## 1. Who will teach my batch, and are they teaching today?

The commonest gap between a demo and a course. The senior person runs the demo, a junior runs your batch at 9 AM.

A good answer names the person, their experience, and ideally has them in the room. A poor answer is "our expert faculty" with no name attached.

## 2. Will I work on live accounts with real money?

The single most important question on this list.

There is a large difference between a practice account, a client account someone else manages while you watch, and an account where you make changes that spend money. Only the third teaches judgement, because only the third has consequences.

Ask to see one today. If the answer is that client accounts are confidential, that is a reasonable position, but then ask what you will actually run during the course, and listen for specifics.

## 3. What does the total fee include, and what will I pay separately?

Ask for the full number, then ask what sits outside it. Common extras are certification exam fees, software subscriptions, and ad budget for your own practice campaigns.

Practice ad spend is the one people forget. If you will need ₹5,000 to ₹10,000 of your own money to run campaigns, better to know in week one than in month three. Some institutes provide a budget, some do not, and either is acceptable as long as you are told.

Our own numbers, so you have something to compare against: ₹45,000, or ₹40,000 paid in full at enrollment, for four months plus a two month internship. The alternative is a ₹5,000 enrollment fee with ₹40,000 due on the first day of class. The fee comparison across Sonipat is in [course fees compared](/blog/digital-marketing-course-fees-comparison-sonipat).

## 4. What are the placement conditions, in writing, today?

Not the percentage. The conditions.

Every genuine guarantee has them, and they are the whole substance of the promise. Ask for the document before you pay, not after. Read it for the clauses that quietly void it: 100% attendance, "any offer in any city", a minimum salary floor you must accept.

Ours, since I am telling you to ask: complete every module, attend at least 75% of classes, finish your live campaign portfolio project, apply to at least 30 jobs through our portal within four months of finishing, and attend two placement drives. Meet all of it and if you are still not placed, we refund the fee in full. Around 80% of our graduates are placed within 30 days, and the full text is on the [placement guarantee](/placement) page.

## 5. How many students in a batch?

Above about twenty five, personal attention stops existing regardless of what anyone intends. For online batches the same number applies, because a live class with forty people is a webinar.

Ask how many are in the batch you would join, not the average.

## 6. What happens when I miss a class?

You will miss classes. Everybody does.

Good answers: a recording is available, there is a doubt session each week, you can sit the same class in another slot. Poor answer: you will have to catch up on your own.

Ask specifically whether you can attend a different time slot. For working students and anyone commuting, this one matters more than it sounds.

## 7. Who answers my doubts at 9 PM on a Sunday?

Learning in this field happens when you are stuck, which is rarely during class hours.

A named trainer group with a stated response window is a real system. "WhatsApp us anytime" with nobody accountable is not. Ask what the typical response time is, then ask a current student the same question and compare.

## 8. What are your last three graduates doing right now?

Not the best three. The last three.

This question is hard to answer dishonestly on the spot, which is exactly why it is useful. Listen for job titles, employers and salary ranges that sound ordinary rather than remarkable. A real answer includes someone still looking, or someone who took a role below what they hoped for.

Any institute whose last three graduates all walked into exceptional jobs is describing their best three.

## 9. What does this course not cover?

An honest answer here is the strongest signal of a decent institute.

Nobody teaches everything. A four month course cannot cover deep technical SEO, advanced analytics engineering, design craft and copywriting at a professional level. Someone who says "we cover everything" is either not thinking about it or not being straight with you.

Our own list: we do not teach graphic design beyond functional work, we do not teach development beyond basic site building, and we do not teach video editing as a craft, only as much as a campaign needs. That reasoning is in [the course syllabus](/blog/digital-marketing-course-syllabus-2026).

## 10. Can I speak to a current student, without you in the room?

The question that separates confident institutes from nervous ones.

You are not asking for a testimonial. You are asking for five minutes with somebody three months into the course, unsupervised. Ask them what they would warn you about, what the trainer is like on a bad day, and whether the course is what they expected.

Hesitation here is informative. So is an immediate yes.

## 11. What happens if I fall behind?

Different from missing a class. This is about the student who attends but is not keeping up, which is common and treatable.

Ask whether there is any support beyond "attend the doubt session". Repeating a module, a slower parallel track, one-to-one time: ask what exists and what it costs. Students from Hindi medium backgrounds should ask this directly, and our note on [studying in Hindi medium](/blog/digital-marketing-course-hindi-medium-students) covers how we handle it.

## 12. What does the refund policy actually say?

Read it. Not the summary, the document.

Specifically: what happens if you withdraw in week two, in month two, and after the course. What proportion is refundable at each point. Whether the enrollment fee is included. Whether any refund depends on a reason being accepted by the institute.

A clear refund policy is not a sign that an institute expects you to leave. It is a sign that someone wrote their terms down properly.

## The answers that should worry you

Having asked all twelve at a few places, these are the replies that come up and what they usually mean.

| What you hear | What it often means |
|---|---|
| "We cover everything" | Nobody has thought carefully about the syllabus |
| "100% guaranteed placement, no conditions" | The guarantee is unenforceable or not meant seriously |
| "Our expert faculty will teach you" | The trainer is not decided, or not impressive |
| "Fees are increasing from Monday" | A closing tactic, not a fee schedule |
| "You can speak to students after you enrol" | No |
| "Our average package is ₹6 LPA" | One outlier is carrying an average |

None of these is proof of a bad institute on its own. Three of them together is enough to walk out.

## How to run the demo so you learn something

A few practical notes, because the format works against you if you are passive.

Go to two or three, not one. The comparison is where the information is.

Take the questions on paper and tick them off. You will forget four of them otherwise, and the counsellor is not going to remind you.

Decide before you walk in that you are not paying anything today. Say so pleasantly at the start. It changes the conversation from a sale to a conversation, and a good institute will not mind.

Bring whoever is paying. Parents ask about refunds and conditions, which students skip. The parents' version of this discussion is in [a parents' guide to a digital marketing career](/blog/sonipat-parents-guide-digital-marketing-career).

And ask to see something that is not working. A campaign that is over cost, an account that lost money last month. Any trainer worth paying will show you one and explain what they are doing about it. That single request tells you whether you are in a classroom or a showroom.

## Frequently asked questions

**What are the most important two questions?**
Will I work on live accounts with real money, and what are the placement conditions in writing.

**Is a demo class really free?**
It should be, with no obligation. Ask when you book, and be wary of anything requiring a token payment.

**How long should a demo last?**
Forty minutes to an hour of teaching. If fees come up in the first fifteen minutes, it is a pitch.

**Should I attend more than one?**
Yes, two or three. Ask the same questions at each and write the answers down.

**Can my parents come?**
Yes, and they should if they are paying. Agree in advance that nobody signs anything on the day.
