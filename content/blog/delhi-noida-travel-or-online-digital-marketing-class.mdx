If you live in South Delhi, Noida or anywhere on that side of the NCR, the honest comparison is not between a Delhi institute and a Sonipat one. It is between three hours of travel a week and a live class you attend from your room.

For most working people the travel loses. For most 20 year olds who struggle to sit down without someone watching, it wins. That is the whole decision, and the fee difference is usually a bigger factor than either.

## Put a number on the commute first

People compare course fees carefully and then ignore the cost of getting there. Over four months the travel is not trivial.

| Three classes a week, four months | Metro | Own vehicle |
|---|---|---|
| South Delhi to a central Delhi institute | ₹1,000 to ₹1,500 a month | ₹1,800 to ₹2,600 a month |
| Noida to a South Delhi institute | ₹1,400 to ₹2,000 a month | ₹2,400 to ₹3,500 a month |
| Travel time, round trip | 60 to 100 minutes per class | 70 to 120 minutes per class |
| Four month total | ₹4,000 to ₹8,000 and 120 to 160 hours | ₹7,000 to ₹14,000 and 140 to 190 hours |

150 hours is nearly four full working weeks. If you are already employed, that is the part that breaks the plan by week six, not the money.

## What you genuinely lose by going online

I am not going to pretend the online batch is identical. Three things are worse.

**Nobody is watching you work.** In a classroom, a trainer walking past sees you about to set a campaign live with the wrong location targeting. Online, that mistake costs you ₹400 and you learn it from the invoice instead.

**The corridor conversation disappears.** A surprising amount of what students learn comes from the five minutes before class, where someone describes an interview they just failed. You can replace it with a batch WhatsApp group, but not fully.

**Low-discipline students drift.** Attendance in our online batches is good because the live format forces it, but the people who quietly disappear in month three are almost always online students, not classroom ones.

If you read that list and recognise yourself in the third point, travel to a class. Any class. The discipline is worth more than the convenience.

Everything that produces the skill.

Same trainer, in the same session as the classroom students, not a separate recorded track. Same live client accounts, since you are working in a browser either way. Same assignments, same fortnightly presentations of your own campaign numbers, same doubt sessions. Same two month internship afterwards, which for Delhi-side students is almost always remote anyway.

Ad platforms do not care where you sit. That is the uncomfortable truth for anyone charging a premium for a classroom in Delhi. ## How to judge any online batch before you pay

Most of the complaints people have about online courses are complaints about one specific bad format: a recorded library sold as a course. Five questions separate the two, and they are worth asking any institute, including us.

**Is the trainer live, in the same session as the classroom?** If there is a separate online track, ask who teaches it. Often it is a junior.

**How many students in the batch?** Above about twenty five, a live online class becomes a webinar and your questions stop getting answered.

**Do I work on live accounts with real money?** This is the whole test. Practice accounts teach the buttons, not the judgement, and the judgement is what is being paid for.

**What happens when I am stuck at 9 PM?** A trainer group with a stated response window is a real answer. "WhatsApp us" with no commitment is not.

**Is attendance tracked, and what happens if I drop off?** The institutes that follow up when you miss two classes are the ones whose online students finish.

If a course answers all five well, the format difference between online and classroom narrows to a matter of your own discipline. If it answers two of five, the price should be a fraction of a classroom fee, and usually is not.

## The practical setup you need at home

Small thing, frequently the reason people struggle, and worth sorting before day one rather than in week two.

You need a laptop rather than a phone. This is not snobbery: Google Ads, Analytics and Meta Business Suite are not usable for real work on a phone screen, and students who try spend the first month at a disadvantage they blame on themselves. A basic ₹30,000 machine is enough, and one of the arguments for the cheaper course fee is that the saving covers it.

You need a wired or stable connection for the 7:30 AM slot, because mornings are when shared home connections are most contested. You need a corner where you will not be interrupted for forty five minutes, which in a joint family home takes some negotiating and is better negotiated in advance.

And you need headphones. Students who attend on speaker get distracted, and everyone else can hear their room.
