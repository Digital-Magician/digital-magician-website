"How long does a digital marketing course take?" sounds like a simple question, but the honest answer has two parts: how long the *course* runs, and how long until you're actually *job-ready*. They're not the same thing, and the gap between them is where most people get misled.

## The Quick Answer

- **A single specialisation** (just SEO, or just Google Ads): **6–8 weeks**
- **A complete full-stack program** (everything): **3–4 months**
- **Becoming genuinely job-ready with a portfolio:** **3–6 months** of consistent practice

If anyone promises to make you a "digital marketing expert in 7 days," close the tab. You can *learn what the tools are* in a week. You cannot *learn to run profitable campaigns* in a week — that takes repetition on real accounts.

## Why It Takes a Few Months (Not a Few Days)

Digital marketing is a skill, like driving. You can read the manual in an afternoon. But you don't become a confident driver until you've actually driven for weeks, in real traffic, making real mistakes.

Campaigns are the same. To be job-ready, you need to have actually:

- Set up conversion tracking and watched it fire
- Launched a campaign, seen it underperform, and fixed it
- Read a Search Terms report and added negative keywords
- Tested two ad creatives and interpreted which won and why

None of that happens from watching videos. It happens from doing — and doing takes time. That's why the *real* timeline is measured in months of practice, not hours of content.

## Specialisation vs Full-Stack: Which Timeline Fits You?

**Go specialisation (6–8 weeks) if:** you already know which path you want, or you need a job fast in one specific skill. For example, a focused [SEO course](/seo-course/sonipat) or [Google Ads course](/google-ads-course/sonipat) in Sonipat gets you deep on one platform quickly.

**Go full-stack (3–4 months) if:** you're newer and want to discover what you enjoy, or you want the strongest, most flexible profile for the job market. The [Full Stack Digital Marketing program](/programs/full-stack-digital-marketing) covers everything, so you graduate able to apply for a wider range of roles.

For most freshers, I recommend full-stack — those extra weeks pay for themselves many times over in interview options.

## Can You Learn It While Working or Studying?

Yes — and most of my students do exactly that. A well-designed course runs evening classes (ours are Mon/Wed/Fri, 7–9 PM) and records every session, so you can:

- Attend live after work or college
- Catch up on recordings when you miss a class
- Practise on weekends

Plan for roughly **6–10 hours a week** — class time plus practice. That's the realistic commitment to genuinely absorb it without burning out.

> The students who finish fastest aren't the ones with the most free time. They're the ones who practise a little every day instead of cramming. Consistency beats intensity here.

## "Job-Ready" vs "Course Complete" — The Distinction That Matters

Here's the trap. Many institutes time their course to end at "you've seen all the modules." But seeing the modules isn't the finish line — being able to *do the work in an interview* is.

A course is worth your time only if, by the end, you've built a real portfolio *during* the course — not after. That's the difference between "I completed a 3-month course" and "here are three campaigns I ran and the results I got." One gets ignored; the other gets hired.

## So What Should You Expect?

If you start a serious, hands-on course today and practise consistently:

- **Weeks 1–4:** foundations + your first real campaigns
- **Weeks 5–12:** depth across platforms, building your portfolio
- **Month 3–4:** applying to jobs (or freelancing) with proof of results

Most of our graduates are placed within 30–60 days of finishing — which means, realistically, you can go from complete beginner to first paycheck in **4–6 months.** That's faster than almost any other career with this earning potential.

## The Bottom Line

Don't pick a course by how *short* it is. Pick it by whether it makes you genuinely able to run campaigns — because a "fast" course that skips real practice just delays the moment you become employable.
